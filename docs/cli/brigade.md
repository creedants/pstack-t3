## brigade.py brief

```text
brigade.py [flags] brief [--fields FIELDS] [--goal GOAL] [--acceptance ACCEPTANCE] [--contract CONTRACT] [--verify VERIFY] [--paths PATHS] [--lease LEASE] [--base BASE] [--context CONTEXT] id
```

Render the worker brief for a dish. Refuses when a field or the coordinator thread is missing. House rules use the case-sensitive tags [worker], [reviewer], and [coordinator]. The first tag anywhere on a rule's first line selects its role. After outer whitespace is stripped, each non-empty line starting without a space or tab begins a rule. Following blank lines and lines starting with a space or tab continue that rule, even after a blank line. A brief keeps untagged and [worker] rules. It removes the [worker] tag and at most one following space. It leaves out [reviewer] and [coordinator] rules with their continuation lines.

## brigade.py pass record

```text
brigade.py [flags] pass record [--pr PR] --sha SHA --verdict VERDICT --author AUTHOR --verifier VERIFIER [--note NOTE] [--same-family] [--report REPORT] [--edits EDITS] [--reviewer-thread REVIEWER_THREAD] [--reason REASON] [--member | --late] dish
```

Records the verdict, and writes one row of threads.tsv for the reviewer and for each child the worker's report lists. The reviewer's row comes from --reviewer-thread and takes its seat from --verifier. The child rows come from a ## Threads section of reports/\<dish>.md, which this command reads and takes no flag for.

That section starts with the heading `## Threads` on a line of its own. A line of it that opens a fence after up to three spaces is not read, and neither is a line until that fence closes. Every other non-blank line of it is skipped when it is plain prose, that is when it starts, after any leading whitespace, with neither a list marker nor a `#`, and holds no backticked thread id. A list marker is a hyphen, an asterisk, a plus, a bullet, or a number followed by a dot or a closing parenthesis.

This example uses made-up ids. A report whose worker used two children holds the heading `## Threads` and then two items, one for a code delegate whose thread id is `mcp:00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001` and whose seat is `opencode-go/step-5-preview-free`, and one for a claims check whose thread id is `mcp:00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000002` and whose seat is `codex/gpt-6.1-sol`.

The three roles a child may take are code delegate, claims check, and architect runner. A child whose role is any of the other four is refused, because dish --thread writes the worker rows, --reviewer-thread writes the reviewer row, and the thread command writes the panel member row. A report with no ## Threads section adds no row and is not an error. A non-blank line that is read, is not prose and is not of that form is refused by its number. An item whose thread id the table would change is refused by its number too. So is a heading deeper than the section's own, and so is a second ## Threads heading while the first is still open, and those two end the scan. A role that is well formed but is not one of the three is refused by its number and named.

Any refusal happens before the pass row is written, so nothing is recorded and no partial row is written.

## brigade.py pass hits

```text
brigade.py [flags] pass hits [--last LAST]
```

Print a per-reviewer table of rounds, passes, pass-with-edits rounds, send-backs, upheld and overruled findings, and the upheld rate, over the last N days.

A row counts for the verifier its verifier cell names, including a --member row and a --late row. Rows are judged in table order. A round is one pass.tsv row whose at cell is at or after the window's start, which is the given number of days back from now.

A send-back is upheld when the same dish's next recorded round is a pass at a different SHA, which is the next review accepting the change. A later round at another SHA that is not a pass upholds nothing. It is overruled when the dish's next recorded verdict is a pass at the same SHA, or when the dish's round-budget decision was answered known limits or drop after the send-back row's at cell and no later non-member row of the dish is at another SHA. A send-back that neither rule settles counts in neither upheld nor overruled. A pass-with-edits round is upheld when edits.tsv recorded the edits for the dish from that row's SHA and that row's report, and counts in neither otherwise. A pass round and a blocked round are rounds and nothing more, and a send-back that is still pending counts as a send-back round.

The upheld rate is upheld over upheld plus overruled, one decimal place, and n/a when a seat has neither. A member row settles nothing for the dish, so the next recorded round is always a non-member row, but a member row still counts as a round for its own seat and is judged by these definitions. An empty window prints no reviewed rounds in the last N days.

## brigade.py spend

```text
brigade.py [flags] spend [--by BY] [--item ITEM] [--since SINCE] [--json] [--tokens] [--t3-home T3_HOME]
```

In text, a wholly missing measure prints its absence words and no counts. In JSON, one absence word is that string, and two are an object of word to count. Without `--tokens` the text ends `tokens not requested. Pass --tokens to read them.` and JSON sets `threads_without_a_token_source` to `not requested`. Token totals need `--tokens`.

A Claude model line prints `reasoning not recorded` when `usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens` is missing, null, negative, a bool, or not an integer. A recorded count prints as a number, and 0 stays 0. Adding two model totals prints `not recorded` for reasoning when either total was not recorded. Output and dollars stay the recorded totals.

Codex and OpenCode turn totals print under the thread's current model selection, so turns taken before a mid-run model switch count under the later model.

`--by reviewer` groups whole items by the seat that reviewed them. Its token lines include the worker's and every other ledger role's usage for those items. An item reviewed by several seats appears under one joined key.
